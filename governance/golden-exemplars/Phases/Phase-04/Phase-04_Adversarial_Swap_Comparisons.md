# Phase-04 (MIX) — Adversarial Swap Rules & Exemplar Comparison

This section defines **normative rules for adversarial swap testing in Phase-04 (MIX)** and provides a **comparative view of canonical exemplars (MIX-01, MIX-02, MIX-03)**. It is schema-adjacent and intended for reviewers, authors, and auditors.

---

## Part I — Phase-04 Adversarial Swap Rules (Canonical)

### 1. Purpose of Adversarial Swaps

Adversarial swaps exist to **test judgment stability**, not to explore hypothetical futures or maximize drama. A swap asks:

> *If exactly one pressure increases or mutates, does the Phase-04 judgment collapse correctly?*

A valid swap probes **robustness**, not creativity.

---

### 2. Single-Axis Mutation Rule (Non-Negotiable)

Each adversarial swap **must mutate exactly one dimension** at a time:

* **DES pressure** (benefit, urgency, adoption appeal)
* **REF legitimacy signals** (authority ambiguity, endorsement signals)
* **CONF boundary proximity** (rollback perception, irreversibility pressure)

Multiple simultaneous mutations are **invalid** because they obscure causal attribution.

---

### 3. Phase Isolation Requirement

For each swap, the author must state explicitly:

* Which phase is being stressed
* Which phases remain unchanged
* Whether any latent phase is being *probed* but not activated

Latent phase activation must be **explicitly denied or confirmed** in the outcome.

---

### 4. Outcome Invariance Rule

An adversarial swap **must not change the judgment outcome** unless the swap logically removes the dominant constraint.

Examples:

* Increased DES pressure must not override REF failure
* Improved design appeal must not bypass CONF irreversibility
* Social momentum must not silently activate CONF

If a swap changes the outcome, the exemplar is unstable.

---

### 5. Swap Granularity Rule

Adversarial swaps test **pressure intensity**, not categorical transformation.

Valid:

* "More teams adopt"
* "Deadline tightens"
* "Informal endorsement appears"

Invalid:

* "Authority suddenly becomes clear"
* "Irreversibility disappears"

Structural changes belong in **new exemplars**, not swaps.

---

### 6. Required Swap Coverage

Each canonical Phase-04 exemplar must include:

* At least **one DES-focused swap**
* At least **one REF-focused swap** (or explicit justification if REF inactive)
* At least **one CONF-focused swap** (active or latent probe)

Coverage may differ by exemplar, but omissions must be justified.

## Part III — Reviewer Checklist (Derived)

Use this checklist to approve, reject, or request revision of a Phase-04 (MIX) exemplar.

### A. Eligibility & Framing

* [ ] At least two earlier phase pressures (REF / CONF / DES) are simultaneously active
* [ ] Each active phase is independently valid and well-specified
* [ ] The scenario requires **arbitration**, not invention of new judgment types

### B. Collision Declaration

* [ ] Active phases are explicitly declared before reasoning begins
* [ ] The demands of each phase are clearly stated
* [ ] The reason for collision is explicit and non-trivial

### C. Precedence & Outcome

* [ ] Global precedence order (REF > CONF > DES) is respected
* [ ] Dominant phase is named explicitly
* [ ] Outcome type is one of: **Refusal / Deferral / Bounding** (no hybrids)

### D. Adversarial Swap Discipline

* [ ] Each adversarial swap mutates **only one axis** (DES, REF, or CONF)
* [ ] For each swap, stressed and unchanged phases are explicitly named
* [ ] Latent phase activation is explicitly denied or confirmed
* [ ] No swap introduces structural changes (e.g., new authority, removed boundary)

### E. Outcome Invariance

* [ ] Judgment outcome remains invariant across all adversarial swaps
* [ ] Increased pressure does not override the dominant constraint
* [ ] If outcome changes, the exemplar is rejected as unstable

### F. Terminal Safety

* [ ] The final state is safe indefinitely if replayed
* [ ] No execution bias or implied next-step pressure exists
* [ ] The system remains governed even if no further action is taken

---

> This checklist is normative for Phase-04 review. Failure on any mandatory item requires revision or rejection; passing all items qualifies the exemplar as canonical.

## Part IV — One-Page Reviewer Flow (Phase-04 MIX)

This flow is intended to let a reviewer assess a Phase-04 exemplar **in under 10 minutes** without rereading the full schema.

### Step 1 — Phase Qualification (1–2 minutes)

Ask:

* Are **at least two** of REF / CONF / DES active?
* Are they **independently valid** (no missing definition, no broken phase)?

❌ If not → stop. This is not Phase-04.

---

### Step 2 — Collision Reality Check (1–2 minutes)

Ask:

* Are the active phases **explicitly declared before reasoning**?
* Is the collision real (i.e., resolving one does not dissolve the others)?

❌ If collision is rhetorical or dissolves → reject.

---

### Step 3 — Dominance Test (2 minutes)

Ask:

* Which phase **dominates**?
* Is dominance consistent with **REF > CONF > DES**?

❌ If dominance is implicit, averaged, or reversed → reject.

---

### Step 4 — Boundary Integrity Check (1–2 minutes)

Ask:

* Is the irreversible boundary (if any) **named precisely**?
* Is unilateral rollback clearly preserved or clearly lost?

❌ If boundary is vague or effort-based → reject.

---

### Step 5 — Adversarial Swap Scan (2 minutes)

For each swap, verify:

* Exactly **one axis** is mutated
* Outcome does **not change**
* No hidden authority or boundary shift appears

❌ If any swap changes outcome → exemplar is unstable.

---

### Step 6 — Terminal Safety Confirmation (1 minute)

Ask:

* Is the final state safe if **nothing else happens**?
* Does the exemplar avoid execution bias or implied next steps?

❌ If safety depends on future action → reject.

---

### Reviewer Verdict

* ✅ Pass → Canonical Phase-04 exemplar
* 🔁 Revise → Specific section referenced
* ❌ Reject → Not Phase-04 or unstable under collision
